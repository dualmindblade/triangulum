## Local and global

The idea behind #1 and #2. Terrain has two kinds of thing in it:

- **Global features** belong to the planet. They are positions on the sphere and they stretch with its
  radius: continents, ocean basins, climate bands, biome regions, where mountain belts run, the river
  network, where lakes are. Their height scales with the planet (`vscale`, R / Earth's R).
- **Local features** belong to the ground under your feet. They are sized in metres, the same on any planet:
  individual peaks and ridges, valleys, hills, gullies, roughness, river channels and banks, beaches, lake
  shores, strata, the finest octaves of a boundary.

T2 writes this as a table in its findings (proposed wording for `DESIGN.md`) and makes it one place in the
code, so that changing the radius (T1) stretches the first list and leaves the second alone. An individual
peak should be able to stand hundreds of metres high on the 90 km planet, inside the world height.

## Planet size

- `sea_level = H/2`, so one half-height (H/2 = 1024 blocks) is the coarsest voxel. The planet's size is
  N / (H/2) half-heights: 128 today.
- N must be a multiple of the coarsest chunk edge (32 × 1024), so the slider moves in steps of 32
  half-heights, up to the 2^20 of `sim::PosKey`: 1024 half-heights.
- **The lead's reading of "a full-scale Earth analog within world height units":** a planet with Earth's real
  proportions when a half-height stands for Earth's deepest trench (11 km), that is, Earth shrunk until its
  relief just fits the world height. Then 1 block is about 10.8 m of Earth, and the planet is 832
  half-heights: N = 851,968, R ≈ 589 km, `vscale` 1/10.8, Everest 818 m, the deepest trench 1,020 m.

| half-heights | N | R | vertical scale | Everest would be |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 128 (today) | 131,072 | 90.6 km | 1/70.3 | 126 m |
| 256 | 262,144 | 181 km | 1/35.2 | 252 m |
| 512 | 524,288 | 362 km | 1/17.6 | 503 m |
| 832 (Earth analogue) | 851,968 | 589 km | 1/10.8 | 818 m |
| 1024 (the limit) | 1,048,576 | 725 km | 1/8.8 | 1,006 m |

A literal 1 m-per-metre Earth (N ≈ 9.2 M) doesn't fit `PosKey` and isn't this mission.

## Boundaries

The idea behind #4. Today (`worldgen/mod.rs`, `surface`, `block_at`) snow, sea ice, frozen lakes and the
biomes are hard thresholds on a smooth field (`temp_c`, `precip`) plus one dither, a single 41 m gradient
noise (`Column::dither`) of ±1.5 °C or ±40 mm:

- Across a gentle gradient, that dither moves the boundary by a long way, so it shreds into islands: the
  scattered snow and polar ice. Sampled per coarse voxel, it aliases into speckle.
- Where the gradient is steep, the boundary is the bake's smooth contour: rounded from afar.
- There's nothing between: no octaves at 500 m, 5 km, 50 km.

The fix Andrew describes already exists in one place: **`worldgen/coast.rs`**. `wiggle` adds octaves of
uniformly decreasing size to a distance, and because its slope stays under 1 it moves the line but can never
make an island. What he wants is that principle everywhere: a boundary is a line, rough at every scale from
the planet's down to the block, and it does not break up into scattered pieces.
